# Revision analysis protocol (written 2026-11-21 16:43 +06:00, before any of these analyses was run)

Context: internal reviews (number audit, leakage audit, mock PhD review; see notes/review_summary) found that the guard
was evaluated only on fault types it was trained on, that the F1 "site-calibrated" thresholds were fitted in-sample,
that inference mixed McNemar/Holm with bootstrap CIs and an ad hoc "8 of 10 seeds" rule, and that routing is an
upper bound. All analyses below are post hoc relative to `predictions.md` and will be labelled as such. Frozen
results (`results/FROZEN_2026-11-20`) are not modified; outputs go to `results/REVISION_2026-11-21/`.
Choices below are fixed now and will not be changed after seeing results; any deviation will be logged here with a
time stamp and reported.

## 1. Inference for the error grid (CODE-test)
- Reported quantity: change in F1 (perturbed minus clean), mean over the ten seed models (as in Table 1, Fig. 2).
- CI and test: 2,000 paired bootstrap resamples of the 827 recordings, the same resample applied to all ten seeds and
  to clean and perturbed predictions (seed RNG 2026). 95% percentile CI. Two-sided bootstrap p-value
  p = min(1, 2 * min((#{d* <= 0} + 1)/(B + 1), (#{d* >= 0} + 1)/(B + 1))).
- Multiplicity: Holm within each error family (same families as before), over conditions x classes.
- Same procedure for the published model (seed 1) alone, as a sensitivity analysis. The pre-specified McNemar results
  remain in the frozen files and are reported as such.
- "No detectable effect" for acceptable processing: report max |mean change| and the widest 95% CI.
- Confident errors: keep the pre-specified definition (p > 0.9 FP, p < 0.1 FN); add a threshold-relative
  sensitivity definition: |logit(p) - logit(t_class)| > log(9) on the wrong side (equals 0.9/0.1 at t = 0.5).

## 2. PTB-XL
- Per-recording probabilities regenerated (`src/ptbxl_save_preds.py`, asserted identical F1).
- 95% paired bootstrap CIs (2,000 resamples of the 2,198 recordings) for F1 change of the published model and
  ECGFounder in each replicated condition.
- Text: the raw-500 vs simulated k = 1.25 agreement is described as an implementation check (circular by construction).

## 3. Threshold analysis (AUROC vs F1)
- Model: published model (seed 1) and ECGFounder.
- Replace in-sample ("oracle") thresholds by cross-fitted thresholds: max-F1 thresholds on clean half A, F1
  evaluated on half B, and vice versa; report the mean of the two halves. Half split: seed 11 (same as guard).
- Add AUPRC (average precision) alongside AUROC.

## 4. Input checks (all calibrated on CODE-test half A, evaluated on half B, target 5% false alarms on acceptable
half-A inputs = clean + filtering + quantization + resampling method)
- Detectors: learned guard (as frozen); range rules as a score (max over features of the empirical CDF of the
  deviation from the clean half-A median, CDF taken over acceptable half-A inputs) on 13 interpretable features and on
  all 32; model-based scores of the published model: mean binary entropy, negative max logit, and (ensemble)
  standard deviation of probabilities across the ten seeds averaged over classes; unions "rules-13 OR entropy" and
  "guard OR entropy" with a common quantile chosen on half A for 5% false alarms.
- Matched operating point: all thresholds at the 95th percentile of acceptable half-A scores. Report half-B clean and
  acceptable false-alarm rates with Wilson 95% CIs, detection per condition with Wilson CIs, and AUROC
  (harmful vs acceptable, half B) for score detectors.
- Generalisation: retrain the guard leaving out each fault family (rate, gain, lead, length) and each fault condition;
  report detection of the held-out family/condition on half B.
- Split variability: 100 random half splits (seeds 0-99); for each, re-estimate the amplitude reference, all
  thresholds and quantiles on half A; report mean and 2.5-97.5 percentiles of clean false alarms and mean harmful
  detection for every detector.
- Site lengths (`CODE_DUR`) re-derived from half A only and compared with the values used.
- Harmful set (14 conditions) is fixed from the error grid as before; stated as such.
- Guard false alarms on normal vs abnormal clean recordings (half B).

## 5. Batch-level monitor (no tuning)
- Features fixed a priori from physics: heart rate (time axis), QRS width (time axis), median log peak-to-peak
  amplitude (gain).
- Reference: clean half-A recordings. Test batches of 10, 25 and 50 recordings drawn without replacement from half B
  of each condition (500 batches per size, RNG seed 2026). Two-sample KS test per feature, alarm if any p < 0.05/3.
  No parameter is tuned. Report alarm rate per condition and batch size.

## 6. Routing (upper bound)
- As before (flagged recordings take the cardiology residents' reading of the original recording), per seed with
  each seed's own entropy for entropy-based detectors; mean over seeds; detectors: guard, rules-13, rules-13 OR
  entropy, guard OR entropy, entropy.
- 95% CI of the gain in mean harmful macro F1 (routing minus model alone) from 1,000 bootstrap resamples of half-B
  recordings (seed-mean), RNG 2026.
- Prevalence: for error prevalence 0.1%, 1%, 5%, expected routed recordings per 1,000 and alarm PPV from clean
  false-alarm rate and mean harmful detection on half B.

33. Mains-frequency check (exploratory method, evaluated once):
 - Score per recording: Welch spectrum of the valid span (400 Hz assumed, nperseg = min(1024, length), mean over leads); for every frequency f in 20-150 Hz the ratio of the maximum power within +/-1.5 Hz of f to the median power 3-8 Hz on either side; the score is the largest ratio at frequencies more than 2 Hz from the site's mains frequency (60 Hz for CODE-test, 50 Hz for PTB-XL) and its second harmonic. Under a time-axis error k the mains peak moves to f_mains/k, so a strong peak away from the local mains frequency indicates a rate error.

67. Step 4: prediction of the rate-error failures from clean outputs (script src/round22_prediction.py; outputs round22/prediction/). Pre-specified prediction, exploratory in status, tested on the step 3 outputs.
 a. Inputs, all already existing: clean probabilities at Chapman-Shaoxing (results/preds_chapman/{published_seed1,ecgfounder}/clean.npy), the thresholds of item 66a, labels of item 48c and the 50 bpm SB definition of item 66d, and VentricularRate and QRSDuration from data/chapman/Diagnostics.csv. No perturbed output is read.
 b. Curves. For each model, the clean flag (p > t) is regressed on the device measurement by isotonic regression (scikit-learn IsotonicRegression, out-of-range values clipped): SB flag, decreasing in ventricular rate, and ST flag, increasing in ventricular rate, both fitted on the recordings without atrial fibrillation or atrial flutter (Rhythm AFIB or AF); RBBB flag and LBBB flag, each increasing in QRS duration, fitted on all recordings. Recordings with a missing measurement are left out of the fit.
 c. Prediction. Under a time-axis error k, a recording with rate r and QRS duration d is predicted to be flagged with probability curve(r / k) for SB and ST and curve(d * k) for RBBB and LBBB. Recordings with AF or flutter keep their clean SB and ST flags. For k in 0.5, 0.625, 0.8, 0.9, 1.1, 1.6 and 2.5, and per model and class (SB under both definitions, ST, RBBB, LBBB): predicted sensitivity (mean predicted flag over positives), specificity (1 minus the mean over negatives) and F1 from the expected counts. k = 1.25 is also predicted as a check against the raw500 results already seen; it does not count. k = 1 is also predicted and compared with the observed clean F1 as an in-sample reference; it does not count.
 d. Margins, fixed now. A cell is met when predicted minus observed F1 lies within plus or minus 0.05. Boundary shift exponent: on the step 3 outputs, the observed flag of each class is regressed on the clean device measurement by the same isotonic method at each k, and the measurement where the curve crosses 0.5 is found (r50 for rate, d50 for duration; no crossing inside the observed range means that k is left out and listed). The crossing is the midpoint between the largest measurement whose fitted value is below 0.5 and the smallest whose fitted value is 0.5 or above; for the decreasing SB curve, the midpoint between the largest measurement whose fitted value is 0.5 or above and the smallest whose fitted value is below 0.5. The exponent a is the least-squares slope through the origin of log(r50_k / r50_clean) on log k (rate classes) or of log(d50_k / d50_clean) on -log k (duration classes), fitted over all k in 0.5, 0.625, 0.8, 0.9, 1.1, 1.6 and 2.5 that have a crossing; k = 1.25 is left out. The prediction implies a = 1. Met when 0.95 <= a <= 1.05; not estimable when fewer than three k values have a crossing.
 e. Expectation, written now. For the Ribeiro model, SB under both definitions and ST at k = 0.8, 0.9 and 1.1 are 9 cells; the expectation is met when at least 7 of them are within the 0.05 margin. The exponent is expected to be met for these classes. At k = 0.5 and 2.5 the same classes give 6 cells, of which at most 2 are expected within the margin, because those errors also change waveform shape and, for k > 1, crop the recording to the input window. No expectation is written for ECGFounder or for the bundle branch blocks, which are reported whatever they show. Every miss is reported.
 f. Order of operations. The prediction CSV is written and its SHA-256 is appended to this protocol as a new item before any step 3 output is opened. The author pushes the protocol with this checksum to the public repository, and the push time is recorded here, before any step 3 output is opened.
